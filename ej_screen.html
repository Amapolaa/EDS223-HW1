III.Create two maps communicating an enviromental justice issue

Mapping the percentage of low-income in San Mateo County, CA:

# Static map
tmap_mode("plot")

sm_lwinc <- tm_shape(county) + # defines the spatial object to plot
  tm_polygons(
    size =0.8,
    fill = "LOWINCPCT", #inputs variable from ejscreen data
    fill.scale = tm_scale(values= c("darkorange","lightyellow")),
    fill.legend = tm_legend(title = "% of Low-Income")
    )+
        tm_title("Percentage of Low-Income in San Mateo County")+
        tm_scalebar(position = c("bottom", "left"))+
        tm_compass(position = c("bottom", "right"))
# Static map
tmap_mode("plot")

tx_air <- tm_shape(county) + 
  tm_polygons(
    size =0.8,
    fill = "RSEI_AIR", #inputs variable from ejscreen
    fill.scale = tm_scale(values= c("-viridis")),
    fill.legend = tm_legend(title = "Levels of Toxic Air Releases")
    )+
        tm_title("Concentration of Toxic Air Releases in San Mateo County")+
        tm_scalebar(position = c("bottom", "left"))+
        tm_compass(position = c("bottom", "right"))

IV. Arrange both maps together

tmap_arrange(sm_lwinc, tx_air)

V. Analysis

When thinking about environmental justice issues, two variables from the ejscreen dataset were “% of low income” and “concentration of toxic air releases” within San Mateo County, CA. I wanted to see whether there was a relationship between the percentage of low-income communities and toxic air releases. I asked this because I lived in San Mateo for the last six years before moving to Santa Barbara. San Mateo County is known for being beautiful and green, with lots of nature nearby; however, where I lived, it was predominantly low-income, with industrial sites nearby and close proximity to freeways. Looking at both maps, I see that areas with a higher percentage of low-income people also have a darker shade, indicating higher levels of toxic releases in the same vicinity. I also see that in the second map, where toxic air concentrations are lower, the percentage of low-income people also drops. This suggests structural injustice affecting disadvantaged communities living in certain areas within the county. My maps do have limitations, they show the county as a whole and combine all areas. In my future analysis, I would like to go deeper into the dataset by collecting more information on low-income percentages and showing what types of toxicity are polluting the air. I believe my maps show that in certain regions of the county with higher levels of low-income residents, there are also higher concentrations of toxic air releases.
